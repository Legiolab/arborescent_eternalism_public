# AE V8.4 — Prefix-factorisation and modal-structure indispensability

**Status:** formalisation memo for the publication draft  
**Scope:** representation theorem, minimality result and interpretation limits

## 1. Primitive data

Let `ℋ` be a non-empty set of complete histories. For each finite resolution
level `n∈ℕ`, let

\[
r_n:\mathcal H\longrightarrow X_n
\]

assign to a complete history its recorded finite restriction at level `n`.
Write `h∼_n h'` iff `r_n(h)=r_n(h')`; thus `X_n` may be identified with the
realised quotient `ℋ/∼_n`.

Assume:

- **A1 (common root):** `X_0={*}`;
- **A2 (projective refinement):** for every `n` there is a map
  `p_{n+1,n}:X_{n+1}→X_n` such that
  `r_n=p_{n+1,n}∘r_{n+1}`;
- **A3 (finite separation):** if `h≠h'`, then `r_n(h)≠r_n(h')` for some finite
  `n`;
- **A4 (genuine alternatives):** for some `n` and `x∈X_n`, the inverse image
  `p_{n+1,n}^{-1}(x)` contains at least two elements realised by histories in
  `ℋ`.

No temporal becoming, concrete plurality of worlds or probability measure is
assumed.

## 2. Canonical prefix object

Define the graded object

\[
T(\mathcal H,r)=\coprod_{n\in\mathbb N}X_n
\]

with root `*∈X_0` and an edge from `y∈X_{n+1}` to
`p_{n+1,n}(y)∈X_n`. A complete history `h` determines the coherent ray

\[
(r_0(h),r_1(h),r_2(h),\ldots).
\]

### Proposition 1 — rooted arborescence

Under A1–A2, `T(ℋ,r)` is a graded rooted tree: every non-root vertex has one
immediate parent and one finite path to the root. Under A4 it is non-linear.
Under A3 the map from histories to infinite coherent rays is injective.

### Proof

For `y∈X_{n+1}`, A2 supplies the unique parent `p_{n+1,n}(y)`. Iterating the
projective maps yields exactly one chain from `y` to the unique element of
`X_0`, proving connected rootedness and excluding cycles because every edge
strictly lowers the grade. A4 supplies a vertex with at least two children.
Finally, if two histories induce the same ray, all their finite restrictions
agree, contradicting A3 unless the histories are identical. `□`

## 3. Adequate representations and elimination

A **prefix-adequate representation** of `(ℋ,r)` is a graded rooted directed
graph `G=⊔G_n`, together with maps

\[
q_n:G_n\longrightarrow X_n
\]

and a path assignment `η:ℋ→Path_∞(G)` satisfying:

1. the grade-`n` vertex of `η(h)` is sent by `q_n` to `r_n(h)`;
2. graph edges respect the refinement maps;
3. every vertex lies on the path of at least one represented history.

Define observational equivalence on vertices of the same grade by

\[
g\equiv g'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_n(g)=q_n(g').
\]

The representation is **extensional** when each `q_n` is injective: it does not
duplicate a recorded prefix merely because of notation, gauge or presentation.

### Proposition 2 — minimal factorisation

Every prefix-adequate representation `G` admits a canonical surjective
grade-preserving morphism

\[
q:G\longrightarrow T(\mathcal H,r).
\]

Its kernel is exactly observational equivalence. Hence

\[
G/{\equiv}\;\cong\;T(\mathcal H,r).
\]

In particular, every extensional prefix-adequate representation is isomorphic
to the canonical prefix tree.

### Proof

On grade `n`, define `q(g)=q_n(g)`. Adequacy condition 2 makes this assignment a
graph morphism. Condition 3 and the fact that `X_n` contains only realised
restrictions make it surjective. By definition, two grade-`n` vertices have the
same image exactly when they are observationally equivalent. The quotient by
this kernel therefore induces a bijection on every grade and preserves the
unique parent maps, yielding the stated isomorphism. If `G` is extensional, the
kernel classes are singletons. `□`

### Corollary 2.1 — elimination criterion

No representation can preserve A1–A4 and prefix adequacy while eliminating all
structure isomorphic to `T(ℋ,r)`. It may:

- retain redundant copies of it;
- encode it relationally, categorically or as modal fibers;
- rename its vertices and arrows;

but after removal of representational redundancy its invariant quotient is
`T(ℋ,r)`.

This is the precise mathematical content of conditional representational
indispensability.

## 4. Independence and the configuration quotient

The prefix tree distinguishes different serialisations. That is correct for
conflicting alternatives but overcounts independent events.

Let `Σ` be a set of event labels and `I⊆Σ×Σ` a symmetric irreflexive
independence relation. Let `≃_I` be the congruence generated by adjacent swaps

\[
uabv\simeq_I ubav\qquad\text{whenever }(a,b)\in I.
\]

The quotient of words by `≃_I` is the associated trace structure. Its finite
configuration order may contain diamonds: from a configuration `C`, independent
events `a,b` give

\[
C\prec C\cup\{a\},\;C\cup\{b\}\prec C\cup\{a,b\}.
\]

### Proposition 3 — tree/diamond invariant

The serialisation unfolding `\widetilde E` is arborescent, while the physical
configuration quotient `Q=\widetilde E/{\simeq_I}` is generally a partial order
with diamonds. A representation invariant under benign reordering must preserve
the quotient map

\[
\widetilde E\longrightarrow Q,
\]

not merely the raw prefix tree.

### Consequence

The publishable AE invariant is not the slogan “reality is literally a tree.”
It is the typed pair consisting of an arborescent unfolding and a quotient that
identifies presentation-level reorderings while retaining genuine conflict.

## 5. Modal fibers

Let `b:Q→M` associate a physical configuration with its base event or region.
For `x∈M`, define the modal fiber

\[
F_x=b^{-1}(x)
\]

or, when continuations rather than configurations are required, the set of
coherent future extensions compatible with the restriction at `x`.

Propositions 1–3 establish the indispensability of these indexed modal contents
for prefix-adequate representations. They do **not** alone establish that
`π:E→M` is a smooth locally trivial fiber bundle. Promotion from a fibered set
or presheaf to a topological/smooth bundle requires additional conditions from
the physical theory: topology, regularity, compatible transition maps and local
triviality.

Thus an alternative notation can eliminate the word “fiber,” but not the
indexed modal content, if it remains structure-preserving.

## 6. Assumption ablations

| Removed condition | Countermodel | Lost conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | two disjoint initial classes | one rooted tree becomes a forest |
| A2 | a later record with two incomparable parents | unique ancestry and tree factorisation fail |
| A3 | two distinct histories agreeing at every finite level | histories are not identified with separated rays |
| A4 | one refinement at every vertex | the structure is a linear chain, not a genuine arborescence |
| extensionality | duplicate every vertex with an unobservable colour | representation is not minimal, though its quotient remains `T` |
| independence quotient | serialisations `ab` and `ba` counted separately | presentation multiplicity is mistaken for physical branching |

The ablations show that the conclusion is conditional and that each assumption
performs visible work. They do not show that nature satisfies A1–A4.

## 7. Comparison with B-theoretic competitors

### Minimal B-theory

A single actual 4D history can represent records along that history. It need not
represent genuine incompatible alternatives and therefore need not satisfy A4.
The theorem does not compel it to branch unless that explanatory requirement is
added.

### Past Hypothesis plus B

A low-entropy boundary condition and a statistical postulate address
thermodynamic typicality. They do not by themselves supply the full
shared-prefix modal invariant. If counterfactual alternatives are added in a
prefix-adequate way, Proposition 2 applies.

### Modal B-theory

A B-theory enriched with a solution space, histories or possible worlds can
satisfy A1–A4. After extensional quotient it contains an object isomorphic to
the AE prefix/configuration structure. It is therefore a different possible
interpretation or presentation of the invariant, not an elimination of it.

## 8. What the theorem does and does not prove

### Established, conditionally

- canonical reconstruction of a rooted non-trivial prefix tree;
- injective representation of complete histories as rays;
- minimality up to quotient and isomorphism;
- necessity of the tree/configuration pair when independence is present;
- non-eliminability of the indexed modal content for representations satisfying
  the adequacy conditions.

### Not established

- empirical truth of A1–A4;
- concrete reality of all non-actual histories;
- unique smooth fiber-bundle presentation;
- laws for weights, measure or actualisation;
- cosmological calibration;
- superiority to a modal B-theory carrying the same invariant.

## 9. Novelty discipline

The construction of a prefix tree is elementary, and tree/diamond distinctions
are familiar from branching-time and concurrency formalisms. The paper must not
claim novelty for those ingredients alone.

The candidate contribution is philosophical and structural: a precise
elimination test for modal enrichment of a block universe, a minimal quotient
result, its mapping to AE fibers, and the conclusion that a matched enriched
B-theory translates rather than removes the AE invariant. A full literature
audit must determine whether this exact conjunction is new.
